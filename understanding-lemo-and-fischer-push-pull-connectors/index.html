Understanding Lemo and Fischer Push Pull Connectors

Jan 15, 2021

LATCH DESIGN

An important design difference between Lemo and Fischer is how the latching mechanism works. When comparing Lemo and Fischer receptacles, how the latch works is almost identical. The real magic happens on the plugs. Lemo achieves this function with an outer shell and latching sleeve. The latching sleeve and outer sleeve work together to achieve the push-pull experience. The Fischer design achieves this same function by altering these two components. The outer sleeve and plug body can be seen to below.

Figure 2: Fischer latch design: Fischer Technical Specification Volume 1 page A-5.

Although the design differences are subtle, the difference in connector performance is drastic. This latch design difference impacts two major connector performance areas; ingress protection and shell size options.

INGRESS PROTECTION

The Lemo latch sleeve goes from the inside to the outside of the connector, creating an opening for particles to ingress. These openings in the latch sleeve limit the Lemo B-series and P-series to a IP-64 maximum rating. On the other hand, Fischer plug body is essentially a tube that only needs to be sealed on each end to become IP-68 rated. In order to achieve IP-68 rated connectors Lemo created a separate product line, the K-series. The K-series is essentially a “B-series” connector with a tube under the latch sleeve.

SHELL SIZES

Lemo has many different connector series, each with different shell sizes. This was done because the latching mechanism did not allow for a “one size fits all” connector. The “K-series” needed to increase in size because the inner tube was added. In the 80’s, Redel (Lemo) connectors were introduced with a different shell size, a modified latch design allowed all components to be made from plastics. Meanwhile the Fischer latching design can be molded in plastic and is IP-68 while maintaining backwards compatibility to the receptacle. Having the ability to pair up a metal receptacle with a plastic connector has many practical uses. For example, in many medical applications the device connector is disposable (plastic) and the console receptacle (metal) needs to be highly reliable.
